A vampire transformation video starts with you as you are, then lightning flashes and you change: pale skin, red eyes, fangs and a cape, in a gothic castle with bats in the air. It is a Halloween effect that works well as a short vertical clip.

The [vampire transformation](/en/ai-vampire-transformation-video) page makes that video from one photo. You don't write a prompt. You get a 10-second vertical clip with music and sound. In our test, it was ready about 6 minutes after we submitted it.

## How it works

1. Open **Video generator → Vampire transformation** in the dashboard.
2. Upload **your photo**.
3. Select **Generate (50 credits)**.
4. Wait on the page, or leave it. The video appears in **Your videos** when it is ready, and you get a notification. If generation fails, the credits come back.

The photo is a JPEG, PNG or WebP up to 2 MB. Behind the page, Alibaba's **Wan 3.0** reference-to-video model gets the photo with a prompt in three beats:

| Seconds | What the prompt asks for |
| ------- | ------------------------ |
| 0–4     | A gothic castle hall at night with candles and a full moon. You stand in your own outfit, smiling. |
| 4–8     | Lightning flashes and you turn into a vampire: pale skin, red eyes, small fangs, a black velvet suit and a cape lined in crimson. Bats circle behind you. |
| 8–10    | You raise the cape across your face and lower it with a smirk as the bats fly at the camera. |

## What a real result looks like

We gave the page one photo of an AI-generated man in a blue blazer, holding a guitar. These four frames are taken at 0.5, 3.5, 6.5 and 9.5 seconds of the result:

![Four frames from a 10-second AI vampire transformation video: a man in a blue blazer in a candlelit castle hall, lightning strikes and he wears a crimson-lined cape with red eyes, a close-up of his fangs, then bats fly around him.](/landing/vampire/frames.jpg)

- **The change came early.** Lightning struck at about 3 seconds, not 4, and the cape, the bow tie and the red eyes appeared with it.
- **The face stayed his.** His face and hair stayed the same through the change. Only the skin turned paler.
- **The photo's props came along at first.** He held the guitar from the photo until the change, and then it was gone.
- **The fangs got a close-up.** The camera pushed in, and he smiled to show them at about 6 seconds.
- **The cape move was different.** He swept the cape open to the side instead of raising it across his face, while bats flew around him and one came close to the camera.
- **The video is ready to post.** It is a 720×1280 MP4, 10 seconds, with sound.

## Choose photos that work

- **One person, facing the camera.** The model copies the face it can see.
- **Good light.** A dark or blurred face gives the model less to keep.
- **Half-body works well.** It leaves room for the cape. Things you hold, like the guitar in our test, can stay in the first seconds.
- **Use photos you may use.** Only animate people who agreed to it.

## Questions

**How long does it take?** A few minutes. Ours took about 6. You can leave the page; the video waits in **All videos**.

**What does it cost?** 50 credits for one 10-second video. A failed video is refunded. See the [pricing page](/en/pricing) for credit packs.

**Is it gory?** No. The prompt asks for a costume look with no blood, wounds or biting. In our test, the only red was the eyes and the cape lining.

**Can I make two people vampires?** Not on this page. It takes one photo of one person.

**What format is the video?** A 9:16 MP4, 720×1280, 10 seconds, with sound. You can post it to TikTok, Reels or Shorts without cropping.
